# Aion Forge Simulation Rules

Version: 1.0

Purpose:
This document defines the behavioral laws governing Aion Forge.

It specifies how reality evolves.

It is not intended to provide implementation details or exact mathematical formulas.

Instead, it establishes the principles, constraints, and causal relationships from which simulations emerge.

Whenever implementation conflicts with these rules, these rules take precedence.

---

# 1. First Principle

The central law of Aion Forge is:

> Complexity should emerge from the repeated interaction of simple deterministic systems over time.

This principle supersedes all others.

The simulation should resist hardcoded outcomes.

Instead, conditions should create possibilities.

Possibilities should create probabilities.

Probabilities should create histories.

Histories should create stories.

---

# 2. Determinism

## Rule

Given:

* the same seed,
* identical simulation parameters,
* identical physical laws,

the simulation must produce identical outcomes.

---

## Purpose

Determinism enables:

* reproducibility,
* debugging,
* experimentation,
* sharing,
* scientific comparison.

---

## Requirements

All stochastic processes must derive from:

```text
Universe Seed
```

No uncontrolled randomness is permitted.

---

## Prohibited

* Current timestamps
* Device entropy
* Hidden random generators
* Non-seeded randomness

---

# 3. Causality

## Rule

Effects must arise from identifiable causes.

Nothing should happen "because the script says so."

---

## Acceptable

A civilization collapses because:

* resources decline,
* instability increases,
* external events occur.

---

## Unacceptable

A civilization collapses because:

```text
Year == 5000
```

---

## Purpose

Maintain internal consistency.

Support discoverability.

Encourage understanding.

---

# 4. Emergence

## Rule

Higher-order systems should emerge from lower-order interactions.

---

## Examples

Galaxies emerge from:

* matter distribution,
* attraction,
* expansion.

---

Habitability emerges from:

* stellar properties,
* planetary conditions.

---

Civilizations emerge from:

* species characteristics,
* environmental opportunities.

---

Stories emerge from:

* accumulated events.

---

## Purpose

Ensure complexity remains explainable.

---

# 5. Time Evolution

## Rule

Reality evolves incrementally.

Small changes accumulate.

Large changes are consequences.

---

## Implications

Stars age.

Species adapt.

Civilizations transform.

Galaxies drift.

Nothing instantly appears fully formed.

---

## Exceptions

Universe initialization.

---

# 6. Probability

## Rule

Probability influences outcomes.

Probability does not dictate outcomes.

---

## Example

High habitability:

does not guarantee life.

It increases likelihood.

---

High cooperation:

does not guarantee prosperity.

It influences trajectories.

---

## Purpose

Prevent predictability.

Preserve wonder.

---

# 7. Thresholds

## Rule

Certain phenomena require conditions to cross thresholds.

---

## Examples

Star formation:

requires sufficient density.

---

Life emergence:

requires sufficient environmental suitability.

---

Civilization emergence:

requires sufficient biological complexity.

---

## Purpose

Prevent unrealistic outcomes.

Encourage layered progression.

---

# 8. Resource Constraints

## Rule

All persistent systems consume resources.

---

## Applies To

Stars:

consume fuel.

---

Species:

consume ecological capacity.

---

Civilizations:

consume matter and energy.

---

## Purpose

Prevent infinite growth.

Introduce trade-offs.

Encourage dynamic equilibrium.

---

# 9. Stability Versus Instability

## Rule

Every system exists between order and chaos.

---

## Excessive Stability

Produces stagnation.

---

## Excessive Instability

Produces collapse.

---

## Productive Complexity

Often exists near the boundary between both.

---

## Purpose

Promote diverse outcomes.

Reflect real-world emergence.

---

# 10. Adaptation

## Rule

Systems capable of change should respond to pressure.

---

## Biological Systems

Adapt through evolutionary processes.

---

## Civilizations

Adapt through social and technological processes.

---

## Ecosystems

Adapt through redistribution and resilience.

---

## Purpose

Allow persistence through transformation.

---

# 11. Failure

## Rule

Failure is a natural outcome.

---

## Examples

Stars die.

Species go extinct.

Civilizations collapse.

Planetary systems destabilize.

---

## Purpose

Prevent immortality.

Generate meaningful histories.

Encourage diversity.

---

## Principle

Extinction is not a bug.

Collapse is not failure.

They are legitimate outcomes.

---

# 12. Feedback Loops

## Rule

Systems influence themselves through consequences.

---

## Positive Feedback

Amplifies trends.

Example:

Technological advancement accelerating further advancement.

---

## Negative Feedback

Regulates systems.

Example:

Resource depletion limiting expansion.

---

## Purpose

Create non-linear dynamics.

Increase realism.

Support emergence.

---

# 13. Scale Dependency

## Rule

Different scales obey different dominant processes.

---

## Cosmic Scale

Governed by:

* expansion,
* clustering,
* stellar evolution.

---

## Planetary Scale

Governed by:

* environment,
* climate,
* geology.

---

## Biological Scale

Governed by:

* adaptation,
* competition,
* cooperation.

---

## Civilizational Scale

Governed by:

* institutions,
* innovation,
* conflict,
* exploration.

---

## Purpose

Prevent oversimplification.

---

# 14. Observer Non-Interference

## Rule

Observers influence initial conditions.

Observers do not directly author outcomes.

---

## Observer Responsibilities

May:

* choose seeds,
* modify laws,
* initiate simulations,
* inspect results.

---

## Observer Restrictions

Should not:

* force discoveries,
* dictate events,
* manually save civilizations.

---

## Purpose

Preserve authenticity.

Encourage exploration.

---

# 15. Story Emergence

## Rule

Narrative significance arises from simulation.

It should not be scripted.

---

## Examples

A civilization surviving repeated catastrophes.

A species thriving under extreme conditions.

A lonely world becoming intelligent.

---

## Purpose

Transform data into meaning.

---

# 16. Preservation of the Unexpected

## Rule

Unexpected outcomes should be investigated before elimination.

---

## Questions To Ask

Is the behavior deterministic?

Is it internally consistent?

Does it enrich the simulation?

Can it be explained?

---

## If Yes

Preserve it.

Document it.

Celebrate it.

---

## Purpose

Protect discovery.

Prevent overcorrection.

---

# 17. Simulation Priority Order

When trade-offs occur, priorities shall be:

1. Determinism
2. Internal Consistency
3. Emergence
4. Understandability
5. Performance
6. Visual Fidelity

Lower priorities must not compromise higher priorities without explicit justification.

---

# 18. The Story Equation

The intended flow of Aion Forge is:

```text
Simple Rules
↓
Interactions
↓
Probabilities
↓
Consequences
↓
Histories
↓
Stories
```

Stories are not inputs.

Stories are outputs.

---

# Closing Statement

Aion Forge does not seek to script universes.

It seeks to create conditions under which universes may surprise both their observers and their creators.

A star's death.

A species' resilience.

A civilization's triumph.

A galaxy's silent drift.

These should not occur because they were explicitly written.

They should occur because reality, given its laws and sufficient time, unfolded that way.

The role of Aion Forge is not to tell stories.

It is to build a reality capable of generating them.
